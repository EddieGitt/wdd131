Site Name

WealthFirst Africa

This name was chosen because it captures two essential ideas: the priority of putting wealth-building first in one's life decisions, and its specific focus on the African context — particularly young Nigerians and West Africans starting from zero. The word "First" also reflects the billionaire principle that financial education must come before spending, before lifestyle, before everything else.

Optional domain: wealthfirstafrica.com

Site Purpose

WealthFirst Africa is a financial literacy and wealth-building resource designed for young Nigerians and Africans aged 18–35 who are starting their wealth journey from nothing. The site provides:

  • A step-by-step breakdown of the first moves billionaires and self-made millionaires make — from mindset shifts to identifying market gaps to building scalable income streams
  • A practical budgeting calculator tool that helps users apply the 50/30/20 rule to their own income
  • A curated resource library of free financial education tools, books, and courses
  • A contact and newsletter signup form for receiving weekly wealth tips

The site is grounded in research showing that 70% of the world's billionaires are self-made, and that their first consistent step is investing in financial education — a resource that remains scarce and inaccessible for many young Africans.

Scenarios

The following questions represent the target audience's key needs:

  1. "I earn ₦150,000 a month and have nothing saved — where do I even start?"

    A young professional in Lagos looking for a simple, actionable first step to stop living paycheck to paycheck.

  2. "What did people like Aliko Dangote or Tony Elumelu do differently from everyone else in their early years?"

    A university student researching how Africa's wealthiest self-made billionaires got their start, looking for principles to apply to their own situation.

  3. "How do I calculate how much I should be saving versus spending each month?"

    A recent graduate wanting a practical tool to manage their first salary responsibly.
